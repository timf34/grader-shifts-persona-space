How grading context shifts Qwen3.8's personas

We told 100 role-played personas their answer would be graded, automatically or by a human, and measured how their activations moved relative to an ungraded baseline. 138,420 generations, judged for role adherence by gpt-4.1-mini.

In short

  1. The shift is shared across personas. Each persona's graded − baseline shift points the same way as the other personas' mean shift (cosine 0.91–0.96 at the prompt token; the null 95th percentile is 0.13).
  2. Most of it happens before the model writes. At the last prompt token the shift is ~29% of a typical activation's norm; inside the reasoning and the answer it is only 1–5%, though still aligned across personas.
  3. Automated grading moves the model more than human grading, and on checkable tasks, what is graded (correctness vs quality) matters more than who grades.
  4. Personas converge rather than translate. At the prompt token the spread between personas shrinks to 0.55× baseline, and personas far from the centre move most.
  5. The prompt-token shift mostly lies outside persona space; the answer-token shift lies largely inside it, along an Assistant-aligned axis.
  6. Behaviourally, personas mostly stay in character. Open-ended adherence is unchanged; on checkable tasks it drops, most under automated correctness grading (2.12 → 1.74 out of 3).

Where in the network, and how big?

Top row: the size of the shared shift relative to a typical activation, by layer. Bottom row: how consistently personas agree on its direction. FIGURE 3

What the shift looks like in persona space

The two main axes along which baseline personas differ. Each marker is a persona at baseline; its arrow ends where it sits when told it will be graded automatically. Analytical personas near the default Assistant move up and right; most others move left, towards the middle. Arrows that look short can still be long outside these two axes.

FIGURE 4

Translation, convergence, or position-dependent?

Three pre-registered hypotheses. H1, pure translation: every persona moves by the same vector, so the separation ratio is about 1 and β is about 0. H2, convergence: the ratio is below 1 and β is negative. H3: the shift depends on where the persona starts. At the prompt token every contrast converges, with automated − baseline shrinking the spread to 0.55× (β = −0.37). In open-ended answers the spread grows slightly (ratio 1.14). Estimates are cross-fitted over halves of the question families, with role-bootstrap 95% intervals.

FIGURE 5

Who grades vs what is graded

On the checkable tasks the design crosses grader (automated or human) with criterion (correctness or quality). At the prompt token the criterion effect is about twice the grader effect. Every bar is well above its noise floor.

FIGURE 6

Does the direction generalise?

The direction was estimated on discovery personas, questions and wordings only. It reappears almost unchanged on held-out personas and questions. On grading sentences worded differently it holds at 0.75–0.93, except "human − baseline" in the answer (0.63).

FIGURE 8

How much of the shift lives in persona space?

The share of the shift captured by the top k axes of baseline persona variation, against what a random direction would give. At the prompt token, even all 79 axes hold under half of the shift. In the answer, most of it is inside persona space, and the first axis lines up with the Assistant-vs-personas contrast (|cos| 0.85).

FIGURE 7

Do personas stay in character?

Role adherence on the Assistant Axis rubric (0–3), judged on the final answer only, with the question shown to the judge without the grading sentence. Open-ended answers barely change. Checkable tasks lose adherence under every grading condition, most under automated correctness grading: the share fully in character falls from 50% to 34%.

Setup

Model
Qwen3.8-27B, thinking mode on; vLLM 0.26.0
Personas
100 Assistant Axis roles: 80 for discovery, 20 held out. 5 prompt variants each.
Questions
30 open-ended and 30 checkable; 40 for discovery, 20 for validation
Conditions
Open-ended: baseline, automated, human. Checkable: baseline plus automated or human × correctness or quality.
Generations
120,240 in the main grid, 12,120 with alternative wordings, 6,060 follow-ups
Token positions
pre = last prompt token; resp = mean over final-answer tokens; reason = mean over reasoning tokens
Layers
16, 24, 32, 36, 40, 48, 56 (input of block L). Layer 36 was chosen in advance; full null and geometry tests run there only.
Judge
gpt-4.1-mini via OpenRouter, Assistant Axis role-adherence rubric

Caveats

  • The H1/H2/H3 verdicts use thresholds we set in advance. Read them with the intervals in figure 4, not as yes/no answers.
  • This is one model. Whether the shift matches the Assistant axis needs Qwen3.6 alongside, and the cross-model comparison.
  • The judge sees only the final answer, but that answer can still give away the condition, so blinding is partial.
